Reference definition

What is Suwaya Time?

Suwaya Time is a software-defined model of time. It treats the cycle from Maghrib to the next Maghrib as one Suwaya day, then divides it into 24 virtual Suwayas: 12 for night and 12 for day. Each Suwaya contains 60 virtual minutes, while its duration in civil time depends on the length of the period to which it is allocated and the model's allocation rules.

Model definition

1. Cycle and unit

In the current engine model, the cycle runs from the current Maghrib anchor to the next Maghrib anchor.

Night phase 🌑

12 Suwayas from Maghrib to sunrise, numbered 01–12.

Day phase ☀️

12 Suwayas from sunrise to the next Maghrib, numbered 01–12.

A Suwaya is a virtual unit defined as 60 virtual minutes or 3,600 virtual seconds. Those virtual units do not imply that each Suwaya always lasts 60 civil minutes, or that 24 virtual Suwayas necessarily equal 24 elapsed civil hours.

Important: Equal unit counts for night and day are a design convention in the model, not an astronomical claim that actual night and day have equal duration.

2. The cycle's temporal anchors

The engine uses calculated anchors for Maghrib, Isha, Shar'i midnight, Fajr, sunrise, Dhuhr, Asr, and the next Maghrib. These create seven contiguous periods, and the model allocates an integer number of Suwayas to each.

3. How are the 24 Suwayas allocated?

The seven periods do not receive equal counts. In the base distribution, the four night periods total 12 Suwayas and the three day periods total 12. The base pattern depends on the selected madhab. In a specific high-latitude band, the engine adds a half-year distribution based on the calculated lengths of day and night.

In the current implementation, a half-year adjustment is used when absolute latitude is greater than 48.56° and at most 66.56°. The model chooses the pattern by comparing the calculated day and night spans. These thresholds and distribution rules are implementation choices, not universal religious boundaries in their own right.

4. What is astronomical, religious, and project-defined?

Astronomical phenomena and anchors

What changes in the sky

Sunrise, sunset, solar position, and actual night/day duration depend on location and date. Their calculated timing also depends on the model and on factors such as elevation or atmospheric refraction when represented. The engine calculates these anchors instead of using a fixed wall-clock schedule.

Ordinary calculations use geographic coordinates, date, and time zone. The engine also has a fallback for high-latitude cases where normal solar events do not occur in the usual sequence.

Prayer times and religious definitions

Maghrib, Fajr, Isha, Dhuhr, and Asr

The engine does not invent prayer obligations or their religious rulings; it calculates prayer times using selected methods and settings. Inputs such as Fajr and Isha angles vary by calculation method, and Asr time depends on the selected madhab's shadow-factor setting. Results can therefore differ among recognized methods and local practices.

In this engine, “Shar'i midnight” is calculated at the midpoint of the elapsed interval between the cycle's Maghrib anchor and Fajr anchor. This describes the software implementation; it is not a fatwa and should not replace qualified religious guidance on a specific legal question.

Project-defined conventions

Representation and allocation

The project defines a cycle from Maghrib to Maghrib, exactly 24 Suwayas split into 12 Night and 12 Day units, and 60 virtual minutes per unit. It also defines the seven-period structure, allocates integer counts to those periods, and chooses the visual notation for the clock.

These are documented design decisions in an open-source project, not the discovery of a new natural unit of physical time and not a new religious ruling.

A distinction at Maghrib: the engine separates the Maghrib anchor used by the cycle from a distinct value named calculatedSunset used by the temporal context for the Hijri date transition. In the documented design, that separate value does not follow the manual Maghrib adhan offset. It remains a calculated value derived from the engine's inputs and implementation, not a direct field observation; consult the specification and source when assessing the effect of a particular calculation method.

5. Why does a Suwaya's flow speed vary?

Each Suwaya has 3,600 virtual seconds by definition. The engine distributes actual civil-time spans among periods and assigns each period a count of Suwayas, so one Suwaya's elapsed civil duration can vary.

Flow speed = 3600 ÷ actual civil seconds per Suwaya

1.00×

A Suwaya lasts 3,600 civil seconds.

Above 1.00×

A Suwaya is shorter than 60 civil minutes, so its virtual minutes advance faster.

Below 1.00×

A Suwaya is longer than 60 civil minutes, so its virtual minutes advance more slowly.

This is a computational comparison between virtual units and civil elapsed time. It does not claim that physical time itself changes.

7. Frequently asked questions

Are night and day equal in length in Suwaya Time?

They have equal unit counts in the model (12 each), not equal actual durations. Their lengths depend on location and date.

Is Suwaya Time an official standard replacing UTC?

No. It is a time representation and scheduling model implemented by the Suwaya project, not an international time standard and not a replacement for official civil time.

Is the Suwaya allocation a religious ruling?

The 24-unit split and the count assigned to each period are software design choices. Prayer-time meanings and religious rulings require their appropriate sources and methods; allocation tables are not independent fatwas.

Why do prayer-time results vary between apps?

Calculation methods, Fajr and Isha angles, the Asr madhab setting, high-latitude policies, and local adjustments may differ. Use settings appropriate to your location and reference.

8. External sources for the time anchors

These sources explain astronomical background and prayer-time calculation methods. They do not imply a single method accepted everywhere; choosing a locally appropriate method may require consulting the relevant institution or mosque.

  • NOAA — Sunrise/Sunset Calculations: background on calculating solar times, including horizon and refraction assumptions.
  • Egyptian General Authority of Survey — Prayer Times: an official example of calculated prayer times linked to the apparent motion of the Sun.
  • AlAdhan — Prayer-Time Calculation Methods: a comparison of common methods and parameters such as Fajr and Isha angles.
  • Fiqh Council of North America — Fajr and Isha Methodology: an example of guidance from a fiqh body within its geographic scope, not a universal ruling.
  • Fiqh Council of North America — High-Latitude Prayer Times: discussion of absent twilight markers at high latitudes and different approaches to handling them.
