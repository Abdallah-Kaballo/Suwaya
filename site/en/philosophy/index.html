Purpose and context

Philosophy & history: why a sunset-to-sunset cycle?

Suwaya does not claim to reinvent time or to continue a specific historical clock. It is a contemporary design experiment: what changes if a day is represented as a cycle anchored to selected astronomical markers and that representation is used for planning?

The problem being explored

Civil time is excellent for coordination, transport, work, and digital systems. Yet its fixed hours begin at civil midnight, and a clock number alone does not describe the Sun's position or whether an activity falls in the night or day phase. Suwaya tests an additional representation anchored at Maghrib.

This is not proof that the model improves productivity or is inherently “more accurate.” Its practical value is an empirical question: does this representation help some users understand timing and plan activities?

Why Maghrib?

Maghrib is chosen as a project convention because it is a recognizable marker with a role in Islamic daily life, followed by night and the next day. This is a design choice, not a universal astronomical rule and not a replacement for civil dates or time zones.

The user-adjusted prayer time and the calculated astronomical sunset must remain distinct when they serve different functions, including Hijri date rollover.

What is known about historical influence?

The project provides no evidence that its design was directly derived from a particular civilization, scholar, or historical device. The existence of seasonal hours or astronomical clocks is not enough to establish influence; that requires primary sources, historical scholarship documenting transmission, or an explicit design record.

These are therefore historical comparisons, not claimed origins.

How to test the idea rather than market it

  • Test whether users understand temporal position and its relation to anchors.
  • Compare appointment recall and task completion against a civil-time interface using a predefined study design.
  • Measure conversion errors between Suwaya and civil time, including daylight-saving transitions.
  • Publish methods, test data, and limitations; do not claim superiority before reproducible evidence exists.
In short: Suwaya is a contemporary proposal for representing time. Historical resemblance alone does not establish ancestry or influence.
